# Content Strategy Memo: Netflix Catalog Trends

**To:** Content Acquisition Team
**Re:** What the catalog says about what to greenlight next
**Data:** Netflix title catalog, 8,807 titles (movies and TV shows, releases up to 2021)
**Scope note:** This data shows what was added to the catalog, not how many people watched it. Treat these as supply-side signals to test, not proven demand.

---

## Summary

Three shifts stand out since 2016: series have overtaken films as the main content format, the US is no longer the main source of new titles, and movies have become shorter. Each shift points to a specific acquisition decision.

## Insight 1: Series now drive the slate, and most series are short

- TV shows made up **27% of releases in 2016** and **53% in 2021**. Series have become the main format for new content.
- **67% of TV shows run for a single season**, and only about 1 in 6 run for two. Most series are being tested as short runs rather than as long-running franchises.

**Recommendation:** Shift the acquisition mix toward series, but commission them as limited runs (6 to 10 episodes, one season) with an option on a second season if they perform. This keeps risk low while still building the series slate.

## Insight 2: Non-US production is growing, and India is the biggest source

- The US share of new additions fell from **42% in 2016** to **36% in 2021**.
- India peaked at about **21% of new titles in 2018**, then dropped to about **7% in 2021**. The 2021 drop is a warning sign, not a trend to ignore: the supply of Indian content dipped after the 2018 peak.
- India has the second-largest title count in the catalog, after the US (about 1,000 titles).

**Recommendation:** Keep investing in local-language content, especially from India, and prioritize Indian and other non-US productions with proven audiences in their home markets. Set a target of holding Indian and international content at 20% or more of new additions, and review the 2021 decline before committing to a multi-year deal.

## Insight 3: Movies are getting shorter, and kids content is underserved

- Average movie runtime fell from about **114 min in the 1990s** to about **94 min in the 2020s**.
- Mature ratings (TV-MA, R, NC-17) account for about **46% of new titles (2017 to 2021)**, while kids ratings (TV-Y, TV-Y7, TV-G, G) account for only about **10%**.

**Recommendation:** Acquire films in the 85 to 100 minute range, which matches the current runtime norm. Increase kids and family acquisition, which is well below mature content in share. This is a gap in the current catalog, though it is not yet clear whether the audience for it is as large. Test this with a small slate first.

---

## Next steps

1. Pilot 3 to 5 single-season limited series from non-US markets.
2. Set the film acquisition brief to 85 to 100 minutes.
3. Pull viewing data (watch time, completion rate) to confirm which of these catalog gaps are real demand gaps before committing budget.

## Caveats

- The dataset covers Netflix only. Prime Video and Hotstar are not included, so these findings do not describe those platforms.
- About 830 titles have no country listed. Country figures use the first listed country only.
- Some titles lack a date_added value (10 titles), so they are excluded from time-based additions charts.
